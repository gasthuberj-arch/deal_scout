Production Post-Mortems • Lesson 0016

LiveView Transport Traps, Service Worker Interception, & Boot Blocking

Four real-world browser and supervision tree mistakes: sticky LongPoll fallback, Service Worker socket interception, cross-session navigate bounces, and synchronous init/1 file hashing.

Tied to Your Mission

In commits 848901c, 9fdf8f4, and 3f258c9, we tracked down why deploys sometimes failed health checks and why browsers occasionally got stuck in degraded HTTP polling, served stale CSS, or flashed warnings when clicking navbar links. Every single symptom traced back to a violated boundary between Phoenix LiveView, the browser's Service Worker, and OTP startup semantics.

1. Mistake #1: Sticky `phx:fallback:LongPoll` Session Poisoning

In assets/js/app.js, we originally passed longPollFallbackMs: 2500 to the Phoenix LiveSocket constructor:

// ❌ BEFORE: A 2.5s deploy restart or cellular hiccup triggers LongPoll fallback
const liveSocket = new LiveSocket("/live", Socket, {
  longPollFallbackMs: 2500,
  params: {_csrf_token: csrfToken}
})

Here is the hidden trap inside Phoenix LiveView's client JS: when longPollFallbackMs triggers, Phoenix writes "phx:fallback:LongPoll" = "true" into window.sessionStorage! That flag is sticky for the lifetime of the browser tab. Even after the 2-second deploy finishes and WebSockets are completely healthy again, every subsequent page navigation in that tab stays degraded on HTTP LongPoll.

2. Mistake #2: Service Worker Catch-All Intercepting `/live` & Caching CSS

Sticky LongPoll immediately exposed two critical bugs in priv/static/sw.js:

  1. Intercepting Non-GET & LiveView Endpoints: Our Service Worker's fetch listener had a catch-all event.respondWith(caches.match(request)...) at the bottom without checking request.method !== 'GET' or excluding /live, /socket, and /phoenix! When LiveView fell back to LongPoll, its POST /live/longpoll requests were intercepted by the Service Worker.
  2. Cache-First CSS/JS Bundles: Putting /assets/css/app.css and /assets/js/app.js in STATIC_ASSETS with a cache-first strategy meant browsers kept serving old stylesheets even after new deployments.
// ✅ AFTER (priv/static/sw.js):
if (request.method !== 'GET') return;

const url = new URL(request.url);
if (
  url.origin !== self.location.origin ||
  url.pathname.startsWith('/live') ||
  url.pathname.startsWith('/socket') ||
  url.pathname.startsWith('/phoenix')
) {
  return;
}

And in assets/js/app.js, we removed longPollFallbackMs: 2500 and added window.sessionStorage.removeItem("phx:fallback:LongPoll") on startup so previously poisoned tabs self-heal immediately.

3. Mistake #3: Cross-`live_session` `navigate={...}` Bounces

In DealScoutWeb.Router, public pages like / and /analytics live inside live_session :current_user, whereas /alerts, /matches, /users/settings, and /admin/system live inside live_session :require_authenticated_user.

In DealScoutWeb.Layouts, our navbar links used <.link navigate={~p"/alerts"}>. According to Phoenix.Component.link/1, navigate attempts a live WebSocket transition within the same live_session. Clicking a navigate link that crosses from :current_user into :require_authenticated_user fails over the WebSocket, logs a client warning, and forces a fallback full-page HTTP request—paying the latency of two round trips! Switching navbar links across sessions to standard href={...} performs a clean single HTTP navigation.

4. Mistake #4: Blocking `GenServer.init/1` & O(N) SHA-256 WAL Hashing

In DealScout.Application, DealScout.Backup.Watcher starts before DealScoutWeb.Endpoint in the supervision tree. Because OTP starts supervisor children sequentially, Endpoint cannot bind port 8080 until Backup.Watcher.init/1 returns!

Originally, Backup.Watcher.init/1 synchronously called scan_replica/1, reading and computing SHA-256 hashes across thousands of accumulated Litestream .wal files (from a 48h retention window)—and repeated that full disk SHA-256 sweep on every poll tick! On Fly.io, this delayed port 8080 binding long enough to fail startup health checks. Moving the scan to {:ok, state, {:continue, :initial_scan}} (`handle_continue/2`) and caching file {size, mtime} made startup instantaneous.
